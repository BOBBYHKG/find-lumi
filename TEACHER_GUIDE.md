# Teacher / reviewer guide — Find Lumi 4.1.0

## Purpose, learners and game goal

Topic: shortest paths and reflection. Primary STEM branch Mathematics; Science through plane-mirror optics and ideal bank shots; an optional mirror-design workshop adds Engineering.

Audience: junior secondary learners and beginner adults without engineering knowledge. Captioned examples introduce angles, normals, perpendicular distance, reflected points and intersections. Formal proof and trigonometry are not progression requirements.

Game goal: open a route to Lumi, reunite in Task 5, then guide Maya and Lumi to the museum exit in Task 6. Lumi is safe with Pip throughout. Finishing the six tasks with support can achieve the story goal while independent LO evidence remains below target.

## The two ABCD outcomes

**LO1:** Given four ideal reflection scenarios with incident paths and normal lines, junior secondary or beginner adult learners **predict** the outgoing direction correctly in at least three first attempts, within ±3°.

A: stated learners. B: predict. C: four ideal-reflection scenarios, two bank shots in Task 2 and two light directions in Task 6. D: ≥3/4 first independent predictions, inclusive ±3°. Angles are measured from the normal. The ball model is idealised without spin.

**LO2:** Given three unobstructed source–receiver diagrams on one side of a straight mirror, with grid, straightedge and intersection tools, junior secondary or beginner adult learners **construct** a shortest one-reflection path correctly in at least two first attempts, with B′ and P each within 0.15 model units and an A–B′ connection.

A: stated learners. B: construct. C: three specified one-mirror diagrams with those tools. D: ≥2/3 first constructions, Euclidean point errors ≤0.15 plus the connection. Two checks occur in Task 5 and one in Task 6. The three geometries are distinct and differ from the Task 4 investigation. The tools are explicitly permitted; pointer precision is not a hidden extra criterion.

Each LO has one assessed action verb. First task/response/timestamp snapshots remain unchanged after edits, reload, changed recovery and guidance. Viewer, pass, investigation diagnostic predictions, map visits, story milestones, bonus designs and recap acknowledgements cannot inflate formal LO scores.

The map shows connected adventure progress, current objective, Lumi and the exit. The visitor pass carries the actual successful source/partner geometry, including when the learner succeeds on recovery rather than their original layout. Decorative Star/Flower choices do not alter the pattern criterion or difficulty.

The pattern reader, catch-operated release and optical access gates are fictional museum puzzle devices. Their geometry follows the stated mathematical models. The museum map is not an application of the reflection theorem to an arbitrary maze.

## Instruction and feedback

Task 5 teaches the full reflect/connect/intersect procedure. In the final Task 6 construction, the palette has neutral unnumbered tool names and a goal; the recipe is available through an optional Hint. This is a bounded reduction of prompting, not a claim of unaided far transfer. The reminder supplies no current coordinates or solution. Its first use per draft is logged in support, persists after reload, and never overwrites the first response or changes the LO thresholds. Here independent construction means learner-selected objects rather than Pip's automatic solution; the record also exposes optional support visits.

Each concept is named before/during play and defined in short worked examples. Examples differ from live assessed cases. The sequence after success is action → visible world consequence → one learning popup → continuation. The popup states the completed action, concept and reason, waits for Next, and permits a separate replay or map view. Supported completion is labelled; a Next click is not proof of understanding.

Factual learning: chunked terms and labelled normals, grid units, mirror lines and B′. Conceptual learning: equal-angle comparison, folding correspondence and distance-preserving reflection. Procedural learning: reflect, connect and intersect using learner-chosen objects. Problem solving: test a proposed optical path, optionally compare alternatives, then construct new working paths and transfer the method to the exit.

Immediate feedback diagnoses the actual error: side of normal, angle error, wrong side of crease, sideways displacement, unequal perpendicular distance, wrong B′ or incorrect explanation. Incomplete responses create no attempt. A wrong assessed submission leads to changed practice geometry; one correct recovery continues. Explicit guided completion is available after two errors. A physically working ray activates its receiver even if B′ is mathematically inaccurate; LO2 still records that first construction as incorrect under its explicit point criterion and provides practice. Already opened passages remain open: an explanation error does not relock the Task 4 shutter, and a failed second shot does not revoke the first catch.

No speed reward or lives gate learning. Optional camera movement, keyboard alternatives, larger text, reduced motion, captions and optional sound support access. Reduced motion applies the same completed device/character states without prolonged movement.

## MDA and narrative integration

Mechanics: viewer assembly, ideal bank-shot aiming, fold/check/insert a patterned pass, real ray tracing, straightedge/intersection construction, map/inventory and character movement. Intended dynamics: discover a useful clue, predict/observe, produce and use an object, see the target person beyond a barrier, gain a construction method for new receivers, then guide both characters out. Rewards are concrete changes in the route and a shared departure.

Tasks 4–5 intentionally share one courtyard but use different operating positions and purposes. Task 4's already solved P is not supplied as the new answer. Task 6 introduces no new compulsory mathematical rule. The optional finite-mirror design workshop starts only after escape, outside the six tasks and formal LO counts.

Progress is largely linear. The new connection between activity and route is implemented; its engagement benefit remains a question for learner observation.

For any same-side case: B′=(Bx,−By); P.x=(Ax·By+Bx·Ay)/(Ay+By), P.y=0; minimum distance equals A–B′. Displayed values are rounded; grading uses full precision.

## Accuracy boundaries and references

The viewer is a horizontal cutaway, checks the central ray against both finite mirrors and wall occlusion, and renders from the twice-reflected observer. Full per-pixel aperture clipping is not claimed. Backward guides use reversibility; the eye does not emit light. Bank shots use an ideal point-centre, no-spin model.

Tested light rays obey reflection even after a wrong proposed bounce. Dashed A–P–B paths are proposals; dashed A–B′ lines are constructions. α is the incoming angle and β the required direction to B, so β need not be the actual outgoing angle on a miss. Shortest-path claims require two fixed same-side points, one flat mirror, one reflection and no obstacles. The optional obstacle workshop grades a working finite-mirror link, not global optimality.
